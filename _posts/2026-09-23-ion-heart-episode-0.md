**Look:** Choose **Unification of Apollo** for the Mech Appearance table. The RX-78-2’s humanoid frame and V-fin can read as an Apollonian halo or stylized sunburst. The table describes cultural design cues, not a mandatory parts list: keep its two eyes, faceplate, shield, and pastel paint. The sun mark could appear as a small decal on the shield. This is a natural choice: the salvaged frame's humanoid shape and V-fin already read as Apollonian, and the pastel repaint and sun decal are the visible signs of the Pilot's refit — no further visual redesign is required.

**Model reference:** The supplied image shows an off-white and lavender body, powder-blue torso and shin accents, mint panels, pale gold V-fin, dark rifle, and lavender shield. Its phrase, **“SAME SPIRIT. A SOFTER TOMORROW.”**, makes a good workshop inscription. The model’s two backpack projections can remain visual details; they do not grant extra systems in play.

## What the rolls mean

- **New-build:** Mechanically, you built the current, operational chassis yourself — it didn’t come to you as a working mech. Fictionally, both the frame and the **Ion Core** are old: a decommissioned war-era hull the Pilot dragged out of a depot and rebuilt from the ground up. Enough of it was stripped, replaced, and reforged that it counts as freshly built, even though its bones are a war relic. Which war, and which side it served, is still unknown.
- **Shepherd:** Plowshare was rebuilt for this on purpose — it tends livestock, guides animals across streams, and fusses over them because that’s the job the Pilot gave it. That behavior is the book’s civilian specialisation, distinct from the Pilot’s broader goal of adventuring. That a frame built for combat can be this gentle is worth noticing.
- **Analytical + Adventure Forth:** The Pilot studies a situation calmly before stepping into it. A useful tension is that a perfect plan sometimes takes too long; when the horizon calls, they must choose between certainty and the leap.

## The starting story

On Apollo, the Pilot learned to repair licensed Mech templates by taking on salvage jobs nobody else wanted. In a collapsed depot at the edge of the settlement, they found a fully intact war-era chassis — dead for years, its unit markings scoured away, its Ion Core silent but intact. Most scrappers would have parted it out. The Pilot spent a season stripping it to the frame and rebuilding it, replacing enough of the internals that the finished machine counts, by any inspector’s measure, as new-build. They named it **Plowshare**, after the old idea of turning a weapon into a tool, and gave it a **Shepherd** configuration on purpose: if a machine built for war was going to walk again, it would spend its second life minding animals instead of hurting people. The weapons stayed bolted on. The Pilot never removed them, but doesn’t plan to need them either.

During commissioning, Plowshare noticed a distressed animal outside the workshop before the Pilot did. It stopped a diagnostic sequence, went out, and gently guided the creature away from a dangerous crossing. The Pilot had programmed a shepherd’s tasks, but the amount of care felt personal. This hull has lived before; what it remembers, if anything, is for the campaign to reveal.

The pair are preparing to leave Apollo-Theta for their first assignment near the edge of the Astral Union. Their intention is simple: go see what’s out there. Begin play at the first planet or settlement generated by the book rather than fixing an entire mission in advance. Its first local problem can reveal what “Adventure Forth” means out here.

### Questions to discover during play

1. Where did the salvaged war chassis come from, and who knew it before?
2. Does Plowshare’s tenderness come from the Shepherd configuration, an earlier life, or both?
3. Why did the Pilot decide that adventure required leaving home? Pick an answer when a scene earns one.
4. Who first says “a softer tomorrow,” and what does that promise cost them?

These are prompts, not established secrets. Let rolls and encounters answer them.

## Pilot’s logbook — Entry 000: Commissioning day

**Planet:** Apollo-Theta  
**Location:** Workshop and departure pad  
**Date / time:** ________  
**Pilot:** Benny Starbuck  
**Mech:** Plowshare

> Today I signed off on Plowshare. Medium frame, three attacks, flight system and all. The paint is almost too clean for the work ahead: lavender and off-white armor, blue across the chest, mint on the lower panels, a pale gold crest. Someone at the workshop asked why I put a laser and a sword back on a decommissioned war frame I’d promised would spend its days minding livestock. I said that I hoped we would never need either. I kept them anyway.
>
> During the last systems check, Plowshare turned toward the crossing outside. A frightened animal had stopped in the path of a service hauler. Plowshare reached it before I had worked out what the alarm meant, knelt, and led it aside. The test report called this “expected Shepherd behavior.” I wrote that down. I am not convinced it explains everything.
>
> The whole frame came to me without a reliable history — stripped serials, no manifest, an Ion Core that had clearly seen use. I rebuilt enough of it that the inspectors call it new. Tomorrow we leave Apollo-Theta. I don’t know yet what’s out there. I hope Plowshare wants to find out with me.

**Ion Core temperament / observed behavior:** Patient around frightened animals; curious around people. *First impression, not a rolled mechanical quirk.*  
**Open thread:** The hull’s earlier life — whose war it fought, and why the Ion Core still runs.  
**Starting state:** Level 1; Shielding 40/40; Sync Bond 2/2; no scars or acquired quirks recorded.
